CASE 01 · BIW tooling

From raw part data to a build-ready fixture

An EV underbody arrives as stamped, flanged sheet-metal panels: longitudinal rails, cross-members, spot-weld seams. The job is a fixture that holds every panel in position while it is welded, and a drawing set a shop can build it from.

  • Locating-pin and clamp layout driven by the part's datums and weld access
  • Kinematic CATIA V5 assembly on a rigid base frame
  • Detail drawings with H7 fits, tapped holes and surface-finish symbols
  • Automated BOM generated straight from the assembly

CASE 02 · Kinematics & clearance

Proving every motion before metal is cut

Clamps swing, weld guns close, operators reach in. Every one of those motions is modeled before anything is machined, so a collision shows up on screen instead of on the production line.

  • Parameterized toggle and pneumatic clamp strokes
  • Weld-gun clearance checked through the full motion envelope
  • Operator reach and loading posture verified with a mannequin
  • Moving bases and shuttles laid out with the tooling in place

CASE 03 · Robot handling

End-of-arm tooling that locates like a fixture

A gripper has to hold a panel as precisely as a fixture does, while hanging off a robot flange. That means light frames, onboard valve packs, and clamp units placed where the part can actually be held.

  • Gripper frames built around a central robot mounting flange
  • Onboard valve packs for clamp actuation
  • Clamp and locator units matched to panel edges and features
  • Racks and dunnage for the panels the grippers handle

03 — Process

How a fixture package gets built

Six steps from the customer's part data to a package a shop can quote, machine and assemble.

  1. 01

    Part data in

    Review the raw customer part data: panels, weld spots, product datums and access constraints.

    → Fixture concept
  2. 02

    Locate & clamp

    Choose locating pins, rests and clamp positions from the part's datums, keeping every weld spot reachable.

    → Unit layout
  3. 03

    Kinematics

    Build parameterized 3D assemblies with toggle and pneumatic clamp strokes that open and close as they will on the floor.

    → Kinematic assembly
  4. 04

    Clearance

    Check weld-gun, clamp and operator motion against the part and the tooling through the full stroke.

    → Collision-free design
  5. 05

    Analysis

    Run structural, dynamic or CFD checks in Ansys and MSC Adams where the design needs proof, not judgment.

    → Verified structure
  6. 06

    Drawings & BOM

    Release GD&T detail drawings, weld symbols and automated BOMs, plus NC/CNC code when machining is in scope.

    → Build-ready package

05 — AI evaluation

Where frontier AI gets engineering wrong, and how I catch it

For 2.5 years at Outlier (Scale AI) I trained and evaluated frontier language models as a mechanical-engineering domain expert. The method: write problems that look routine but hide a validity check, and see whether the model notices before it answers with confidence.

Trap 01 Compressible flow

Is the converging nozzle choked?

Check Below the critical pressure ratio (≈ 0.528 for air) the throat chokes and mass flow stops depending on back pressure.

Miss it The answer is confident and non-physical.

Trap 02 Pressure vessels

Is the wall really thin? r/t < 10

Check Thin-wall hoop stress pr/t only holds for thin walls. Thick walls need the Lamé solution.

Miss it Peak stress at the bore is underestimated.

Trap 03 Heat transfer

Does lumped capacitance apply? Bi > 0.1

Check Above Bi ≈ 0.1 the part is no longer one temperature; internal gradients matter.

Miss it The predicted cooling time is wrong.

Trap 04 FEA

Converged, or singular?

Check At a sharp re-entrant corner or point load, peak stress keeps rising with every mesh refinement.

Miss it A "converged" peak value that means nothing.

Trap 05 CFD

Does y+ match the wall treatment?

Check Wall functions want y+ of roughly 30–300; resolving the viscous sublayer needs y+ ≈ 1.

Miss it Wall shear and heat transfer drift off.

Trap 06 Multibody dynamics

Are there redundant constraints?

Check Over-constrained joints make reaction loads non-unique, even when the motion looks right.

Miss it Bearing and joint loads are arbitrary.

Weighted rubrics for open-ended design problems: bolted-joint sizing, shaft fatigue with Goodman and Gerber criteria, pump selection with NPSH checks. Every criterion is pass/fail, so different graders reach the same score.
